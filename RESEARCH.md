# Research basis

MyHSK keeps the interface small while applying a deeper learning model underneath it.

## Why networks matter

Modern Chinese vocabulary is strongly based on compounding. Morphological awareness and vocabulary knowledge jointly support L2 Chinese reading, and grapho-morphological knowledge helps learners infer unfamiliar words.

- https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.736933
- https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1131913
- https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.757934

MyHSK therefore teaches productive character-centered word webs, sound families, meaning-component families, collocations, and scenario packs.

## Why packs are not giant synonym lists

Research has repeatedly found that introducing many new words with closely overlapping meanings can increase within-set confusion. Thematic situations and productive morphology are useful, but near-synonyms should first receive distinct contexts and later be compared deliberately.

- https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716403000195
- https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263118000219

MyHSK uses Contrast Labs after contextual exposure rather than treating related words as interchangeable.

## Whole character first

Most modern characters are phonetic-semantic compounds, so components can provide meaning and sound clues. However, excessive colored radical markings and simultaneous stroke animation can overload learners and reduce whole-character recognition.

- https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.783964
- https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.783613

MyHSK shows the full character first and reveals analysis or writing support on demand.

## Retrieval, context, and production

Each pack uses one guided sequence:

1. Overview the network.
2. Learn a few anchors deeply.
3. Retrieve the full set.
4. Read it across several contexts.
5. Produce one original sentence.
6. Review weak skills separately.

This avoids passive list browsing while keeping the learner free to continue through unlimited packs.
